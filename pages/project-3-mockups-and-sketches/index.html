Step 1: Paper Sketch

Based on the various features and pages you described in the feature spec, draw up sketches of each view. Napkin style quick sketches are a good place to start. Focus more on thinking through the common actions that the users will be making rather than how and where the buttons go. As you draw out these sketches with your team several things will come to light, features you might have missed or interactions that need to be changed.

Time to take the abstract to concrete. Sketch rough layouts of screens focused on features.

Generate options as a group

  • Determine which (2-5) main features you will sketch that are essential to your product (e.g. the feed, leaderboard, comments, etc)
  • Get thick markers, printer paper, and designate a timekeeper
  • Allow for 5 minutes of individual sketching per feature, working on as many unfiltered iterations as possible in that time limit. Treat this much like improv - try not to self-edit or rule out anything just yet.
  • After each round, compare sketches and create a few group versions that incorporates the best of all the ideas. Actively agree/disagree with specific elements. Aim for 2-3 versions per feature.

Rules

  • Use the "thick marker trick" - the thicker the marker, the more you force yourself to keep the designs at a conceptual level the better. Whiteboard markers are great here too, just take photos!
  • Include little or no specific content
  • Drop any worries about scale, straight lines, grids, etc.
  • Think in flows whenever possible (what views or steps come after?)
  • Match your sketches with your flow diagram - what part of the flow is not yet in the sketch?
  • Never settle for your first idea!

You can download all sorts of device paper to help.

Step 2: AI-Generated First Pass

Use AI to rapidly generate and iterate on your mockups. This gets you from paper sketches to digital screens quickly, giving you a foundation to refine.

Choose Your AI Prototyping Tool

  • Text-to-UI generation - describe what you need and get instant layouts
  • Upload your own design system for consistent branding
  • Export to React/Tailwind code or Figma
  • Multiplayer canvas for real-time team collaboration
  • Chrome extension to capture UI inspiration from any website

Fun fact: MagicPatterns was built by Dartmouth alums Teddy Ni and Alex Danilowicz (CS52 and CS98). It's now a YC-backed company used by thousands of product teams.

Using Figma Make? Set up your Figma account first.

  • Built into Figma - stays in your existing workflow
  • Natural language to interactive prototypes
  • "Pilot not co-pilot" philosophy - everything stays fully editable
  • Pull styling from your existing Figma libraries
  • Automatically creates component states, interactions, and user flows

Generate Your Screens

Use your AI tool to generate screens for all the views and functionality in your feature spec.

  • Start from your feature spec - don't just generate random screens. Use the user flows and features you already defined to guide your prompts.
  • Reference your paper sketches - describe the layouts you sketched to the AI, or upload photos as reference.
  • Cover all key screens - onboarding, main views, detail views, settings, error states, empty states, etc.
  • Think about the user journey - what is the action on each screen? What screens are missing to make the experience complete?

Iterate Before Moving On

AI-generated designs are a starting point, not the final product. Your job is to shape them.

  • Iterate, don't accept the first output - run at least 2-3 iterations per major screen. Refine your prompts, adjust layouts, and improve the UX.
  • Critique the AI output - what did it get wrong? Are the touch targets big enough? Is the information hierarchy clear? Does the flow make sense?
  • Edit directly - both tools let you modify the generated designs. Move things around, change copy, fix spacing.
  • Discuss with your team - get feedback from teammates on what works and what doesn't.
Remember: This is a prototype, not production code. The goal is to validate your concept and get feedback, not to ship. Focus on demonstrating the experience, not pixel-perfect polish.

Step 3: Refine in Figma

Now take your AI-generated screens and polish them in Figma. This is where you make it yours - refining the visual design, ensuring consistency, and adding the details that make your product feel complete.

Set Up Figma

Note: Make sure to create your project in the team - and don't create multiple projects — just one project so you can link your various designs together.

There's a short tutorial for figma available as well.

Import and Organize

  • Export from your AI tool - MagicPatterns exports directly to Figma. For Figma Make, your designs are already there.
  • Upload your paper sketches - add photos of your paper sketches to a separate page in your Figma project for reference.
  • Organize your frames - group screens by flow (onboarding, main app, settings, etc.)

Polish Your Designs

  • Establish visual consistency - create a simple design system with consistent colors, typography, and spacing.
  • Refine the details - fix alignment issues, improve copy, ensure adequate contrast.
  • Add missing states - loading states, error messages, empty states, hover effects.
  • Consider accessibility - is text readable? Are touch targets large enough?

Step 4: Make It Interactive

Connect your screens into a clickable, playable prototype.

  • Link screens together - use Figma's prototyping features to connect buttons to their destination screens.
  • Show the core user flows - someone should be able to click through your main use cases.
  • Add transitions - simple animations help communicate how screens relate to each other.
  • Test it yourself - click through the entire flow. Does it make sense? Are any screens missing?
